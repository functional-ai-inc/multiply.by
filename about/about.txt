<h2>Learned everywhere</h2>
What children are expected to memorize depends mostly on where they grow up. In different 
countries, religions and cultures, a child may recite prayers, learn poems by national writers, 
say a pledge of allegiance every morning, or sing the national anthem.

But there is one memorization task that is the same everywhere: the multiplication table. 
Children learn it at least up to 9; in Britain they go <a href="https://multiply.by/base/?12">up to 12</a>, and in India often
<a href="https://multiply.by/base/?20">up to 20</a>.

<h2>How the site and the app were built</h2>
Every page has a SOURCE button in the upper right corner. It opens the page's prompt: the plain
text given to an AI model, which writes the single file <code>index.html</code> that your browser
shows. (Despite its name, the games' <code>index.html</code> is mostly JavaScript and CSS: only about a tenth of it is HTML.)
The Android app is a copy of the site, with everything it needs to work offline.

<h2>Portability</h2>
The build instructions are written to work with models from Google, Anthropic or OpenAI, and
with open-weights models like Kimi. On cheaper models some glitches can be expected.

<h2>Scalability</h2>
The site is static, with no accounts and no server code, so it can cheaply handle large loads.
Each game is a single small file that loads quickly even on a slow connection and costs almost
nothing to serve, so the number of players can grow without slowing the site down. The Android
app works offline.
